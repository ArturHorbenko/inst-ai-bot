# Format: Three Hacks for X

> A fast-paced, high-value format that presents three distinct and practical tips or tricks to solve a common problem or improve a process the audience regularly encounters. Each hack is demonstrated visually as a quick mini-tutorial, providing immediate, actionable value.

**Duration:** 30-60 seconds

## When to Use
Use this format to share actionable advice on a specific, common task or problem your audience faces. It's excellent for building authority, providing tangible value, and creating content that has a high likelihood of being saved and shared.

## Target Audience
Software engineers, developers, and tech professionals who appreciate efficient, practical advice to improve their coding workflows, career skills, or productivity. This format appeals to viewers who want to learn something useful quickly without any fluff.

**Works well for:** Productivity & Workflow, Coding & Debugging, Career Advice & Interview Prep, Developer Tools & Setups, Corporate Life & Communication

## What Makes It Work
The format provides fast, practical, and memorable solutions. Offering exactly three tips feels valuable without being overwhelming. This high utility-to-time ratio encourages viewers to save the video for later reference, signaling value to the algorithm.

### Section Details

**1. Hook** (opening, 1-3 seconds)
State the promise of the video directly and concisely. Use the exact phrase 'Here's three hacks for [topic]' to immediately set expectations and create curiosity.
- *Visual:* Direct-to-camera shot or a visually engaging shot of the subject (e.g., a terminal, a calendar). Text overlay should match the spoken hook.
- *Audio:* Clear, confident voiceover. An engaging sound effect can punctuate the title card.
- *Text overlay:* Title Card: '3 HACKS FOR [X]'

**2. Demonstration** (middle, Under 20 seconds)
Present the first hack. Visually demonstrate the tip in action, showing the process from start to finish. Explain it clearly and quickly with a voiceover.
- *Visual:* Use screen recordings for software tips or dynamic shots for physical actions. Quick cuts are essential. Use a text overlay to label the hack.
- *Audio:* Concise voiceover explaining the 'what' and 'why' of the hack.
- *Text overlay:* Numbered Label: '#1 - [Name of Hack]'

**3. Demonstration** (middle, Under 20 seconds)
Present the second hack. It must be distinct from the first. Visually demonstrate the tip, maintaining the fast pace.
- *Visual:* Maintain visual consistency with the first hack. Use a clear transition to signal the new tip.
- *Audio:* Continue the concise voiceover.
- *Text overlay:* Numbered Label: '#2 - [Name of Hack]'

**4. Demonstration** (middle, Under 20 seconds)
Present the third and final hack, ensuring it offers a different kind of solution or approach from the previous two. Demonstrate it clearly.
- *Visual:* Consistent visual style. A clear transition introduces the final tip.
- *Audio:* Maintain energy in the voiceover through the final tip.
- *Text overlay:* Numbered Label: '#3 - [Name of Hack]'

**5. Payoff** (closing, 3-5 seconds)
Rapidly show the final, successful result of all three hacks. This provides a satisfying conclusion and proves that the tips are effective and deliver on the hook's promise.
- *Visual:* A quick montage or split-screen showing the 'after' state for all three demonstrated hacks. This should be purely visual with no new information.
- *Audio:* Upbeat music swells. A final satisfying sound effect can be used. Voiceover is optional, but if used, it should be a single concluding phrase.


## Pacing
The pacing must be quick and energetic. Move from one hack to the next without any filler. Each hack is its own self-contained, rapidly delivered mini-tutorial.

## Hook Mechanics
The hook is a direct promise using a numbered list: 'Here's three hacks for X'. This works by immediately clarifying the video's value and creating an 'open loop' that makes the viewer want to stay to see all three distinct solutions.

## Visual Style
Clean, clear, and focused on demonstration. Use screen recordings, top-down shots, or direct-to-camera views that best illustrate the hack. Emphasize quick cuts and bold text overlays to maintain a high tempo.

## Audio Strategy
A clear and direct voiceover is the primary audio source, explaining each step concisely. This should be layered over non-distracting, upbeat background music to drive the pace.

## Example Topics
- 3 Git commands that will save you hours of debugging
- 3 hacks for surviving back-to-back Zoom meetings
- 3 ways to answer 'What's your biggest weakness?' in a tech interview
- 3 terminal tricks that make you look like a 10x engineer
- 3 small habits that help fight imposter syndrome as a developer
