# Format: The Gentle Reminder

> An educational format that reminds viewers to perform a specific, important, but often-ignored task. The video quickly demonstrates the exact process to fix or prevent a common problem, often one the viewer wasn't even aware they had, making it highly practical and saveable.

**Duration:** 10-20 seconds

## When to Use
Use this format to share high-value, low-effort tips that solve a common or unknown problem. It's extremely effective for generating saves and establishing credibility as a helpful, knowledgeable resource in your niche.

## Target Audience
Software engineers, tech leads, and engineering managers looking to share practical, actionable advice that solves common but overlooked problems in the tech world. It's for creators who want to build authority by providing genuine, bite-sized utility.

**Works well for:** Developer productivity & workflow hacks, Code hygiene & best practices, Cybersecurity basics, Career development & admin, Digital wellness & burnout prevention, Hardware maintenance for developers

## What Makes It Work
It provides a simple, actionable solution to a problem the viewer either ignores or didn't know they had. This combination of revealing a hidden problem and providing an immediate, easy fix creates immense value and makes the content highly saveable.

## Structure

| # | Role | Duration | What to Do |
|---|------|----------|------------|
| 1 | Hook | 1-3 seconds | Immediately state the reminder using the formula: 'This is your reminder to [SPECIFIC ACTION] to avoid [NEGATIVE OUTCOME]'. This creates instant curiosity and a sense of urgency about a problem they might not know they have. |
| 2 | Demo | 7-15 seconds | Visually demonstrate the exact, step-by-step solution. Show, don't tell. The process should be simple, clear, and easy for the viewer to replicate immediately after watching. |
| 3 | Payoff | 1-2 seconds | End on a clear, satisfying visual of the completed task or the positive result. This provides a sense of closure and reinforces the value of the action, making the viewer feel equipped to solve the problem themselves. |


### Section Details

**1. Hook** (opening, 1-3 seconds)
Immediately state the reminder using the formula: 'This is your reminder to [SPECIFIC ACTION] to avoid [NEGATIVE OUTCOME]'. This creates instant curiosity and a sense of urgency about a problem they might not know they have.
- *Visual:* Start with a wide or establishing shot of the context (e.g., you at your desk, an IDE open on screen) before moving into the action.
- *Audio:* A clear, direct voiceover delivering the hook line. No music yet.
- *Text overlay:* Hook text appears immediately on screen: 'This is your reminder to...'

**2. Demo** (middle, 7-15 seconds)
Visually demonstrate the exact, step-by-step solution. Show, don't tell. The process should be simple, clear, and easy for the viewer to replicate immediately after watching.
- *Visual:* Use quick cuts, screen recordings, or close-up shots to clearly show each step of the process. Keep the visuals clean and focused on the task.
- *Audio:* Use ASMR-style sounds of the action (keyboard typing, mouse clicks) or a subtle, non-distracting instrumental track. The voiceover should be minimal or absent here.
- *Text overlay:* Use concise text overlays to label key steps, tools, or timings (e.g., 'Step 1: Open Terminal', 'Run `npm cache clean --force`', 'Wait 10 seconds').

**3. Payoff** (closing, 1-2 seconds)
End on a clear, satisfying visual of the completed task or the positive result. This provides a sense of closure and reinforces the value of the action, making the viewer feel equipped to solve the problem themselves.
- *Visual:* A static shot of the 'after' state: a cleared cache confirmation, a clean terminal, an organized file structure, etc.
- *Audio:* A simple, satisfying sound effect (e.g., a chime, a click, a whoosh) to confirm completion.


## Pacing
The pacing is rapid and direct. Jump straight from the hook into the demonstration with no wasted time. Each cut should correspond to a new step in the process.

## Hook Mechanics
The hook leverages the 'Oh crap, I should be doing that' feeling. Using the word 'reminder' implies this is common knowledge the viewer has forgotten, creating instant curiosity and a slight fear of missing out on a best practice.

## Visual Style
The overall aesthetic is authentic and utilitarian. It should feel like a colleague sharing a quick tip, not a polished advertisement. Use screen recordings for code/software tips and simple, well-lit direct shots for physical tasks.

## Audio Strategy
Voiceover-led hook followed by task-focused ambient sounds (ASMR) or a low-volume instrumental track. The goal is to let the clear visuals and on-screen text do the teaching after the initial hook.

## Common Mistakes
Making it too long or complicated. The power of this format is its simplicity and immediate actionability. Avoid lengthy explanations, unnecessary steps, or a slow intro.

## Example Topics
- This is your reminder to clear your npm cache to fix weird dependency issues.
- This is your reminder to update your personal 'brag document' after a big launch so you're ready for performance reviews.
- This is your reminder to wipe down your keyboard weekly to avoid Cheeto dust-related bugs.
- This is your reminder to regularly prune merged local git branches to keep your repository clean.
- This is your reminder to take a 5-minute break to stretch your wrists and back to avoid long-term strain.
