# Format: The Expert Reacts (Duet/Stitch)

> This format involves reacting to another creator's video (a 'stitch' or 'duet') to provide expert commentary, humorous critique, or a contrarian take. You leverage the original video's proven ability to stop the scroll, then add your unique value on top, positioning yourself as an authority or a relatable voice.

**Duration:** 30-60 seconds

## When to Use
Use this format to quickly capitalize on a trending topic or viral video in your niche. It's perfect for debunking misinformation, offering a nuanced perspective on a hot-button issue, or establishing your expertise by correcting a common mistake.

## Target Audience
Software engineers, tech leads, and corporate professionals who want to quickly comment on industry trends, debunk misinformation, or share a humorous take on tech culture without having to create a hook from scratch.

**Works well for:** Debunking bad coding advice, Critiquing 'Day in the Life' videos, Reacting to hustle culture or toxic productivity content, Analyzing viral tech news or product announcements, Correcting misconceptions about software engineering

## What Makes It Work
It delivers two pieces of content for the price of one: the curiosity-piquing original clip and the value-added commentary. This structure creates a compelling narrative loop of problem (the original video) and resolution (your expert take), which is highly satisfying for the viewer.

## Structure

### Section Details

**1. Hook** (opening, 40-60% of total)
Show a clip from a viral, controversial, or technically questionable video relevant to your niche. This clip is the hook; it should be inherently scroll-stopping because it's absurd, wrong, or highly relatable.
- *Visual:* Can be presented as a side-by-side 'duet' or as a full-screen 'stitch' that plays before your clip appears.
- *Audio:* Use the original audio from the clip. Let it play out to establish the full context for your reaction.

**2. Build** (middle, 30-50% of total)
Cut to yourself and deliver your commentary. This is where you add value by providing expert context, a funny observation, a personal anecdote, or a direct refutation of the original video's premise.
- *Visual:* If using the 'duet' style, you are visible reacting non-verbally during the hook. If 'stitch' style, this is a hard cut to you speaking to the camera.
- *Audio:* Speak clearly with a confident or humorous tone. The original clip's audio should be muted or faded to a very low volume.
- *Text overlay:* Use captions to summarize your key points as you make them (e.g., '❌ This is why you DON'T use a for-loop here').

**3. Payoff** (closing, 10-20% of total)
Deliver the final punchline or ultimate takeaway. This should be the most memorable and quotable part of your video, summarizing your entire perspective in a single, powerful statement.
- *Visual:* Maintain direct eye contact with the camera to drive the point home.
- *Audio:* A subtle sound effect (like a 'ding' for a good point or a 'buzz' for a bad one) can emphasize the payoff.
- *Text overlay:* Display your final verdict or key lesson as a large, bold title on screen.


## Pacing
The pacing is dictated by the original clip, which is often fast. Your reaction should be concise and punchy, getting straight to the point without unnecessary filler.

## Hook Mechanics
The hook is borrowed directly from a piece of content that is already proven to be viral or controversial. This external validation removes the burden of creating a hook yourself, guaranteeing the first few seconds are engaging.

## Visual Style
The format has two main visual styles: 'Duet' (a split-screen showing you reacting in real-time) or 'Stitch' (the original clip plays first, then it cuts to you). The 'Stitch' is often more effective for delivering a focused, structured takedown.

## Audio Strategy
A combination of the original video's audio followed by your own direct-to-camera voiceover. This creates a clear 'before' and 'after' for the viewer to follow, contrasting the original premise with your expert take.

## Common Mistakes
Reacting without adding clear value. Don't just say 'this is wrong' or laugh; explain *why* it's wrong, offer a better alternative, or connect it to a larger, more meaningful lesson for your audience.

## Example Topics
- Reacting to a viral TikTok of someone 'coding' a website using only a Canva template.
- Debunking a finance influencer's terrible career advice for software engineers.
- A senior dev reacting to a junior dev's overly complex solution for a simple problem.
- Reacting to a clip about 'the death of coding' due to AI and explaining the reality.
- Sharing a personal story about burnout in response to a 'rise and grind' motivational video.
