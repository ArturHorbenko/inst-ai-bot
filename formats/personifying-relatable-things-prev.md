# Format: Personifying Relatable Things

> A comedy skit format where a creator personifies an invisible concept, inanimate object, or abstract system that their audience deals with daily. The format plays out as a dialogue between a regular person and the now-personified thing, escalating from a normal interaction to an absurd but painfully relatable conclusion.

**Duration:** 30-60 seconds

## When to Use
Use this format to make a common pain point or shared experience within your niche feel seen and understood. It's excellent for creating highly relatable and shareable content that builds community through humor.

## Target Audience
Content creators in any niche who want to create relatable, shareable comedy skits. It is ideal for those looking for a low-production, high-impact format that doesn't require fancy equipment or multiple actors.

**Works well for:** Comedy, Technology, Lifestyle, Workplace Humor, Everyday Frustrations

## What Makes It Work
The format's effectiveness comes from its extreme relatability. It gives a voice and face to abstract frustrations (like an algorithm or bad Wi-Fi) that people deal with daily but can't confront, creating a cathartic and hilarious shared experience.

### Section Details

**1. Hook** (opening, 3-5 seconds)
Establish a normal, relatable scenario where a person is interacting with an invisible or inanimate object. Immediately introduce the personified version of that object, creating an intriguing 'what if' scenario that signals a comedy skit.
- *Visual:* Simple shot of the creator as the 'user'. A quick cut or reveal introduces the second character (the personified object), often played by the same creator with a simple prop like a mask or a change in angle.
- *Audio:* Dialogue-focused. The opening lines should quickly establish the two characters and the central conflict.
- *Text overlay:* Label the personified object to clarify the premise (e.g., 'The Algorithm', 'My Wi-Fi').

**2. Build** (middle, 20-45 seconds)
Develop the conversation between the user and the personified object. Start the dialogue relatively normally, then have the object's logic and reasoning become increasingly absurd and frustrating. Escalate the tension and the stakes of the conversation throughout this section.
- *Visual:* Cut back and forth between the two characters. Use close-ups to emphasize reactions of frustration, confusion, or villainous delight.
- *Audio:* The dialogue should carry the humor. Consider adding a subtle, suspenseful or dramatic music bed to heighten the comedic tension of the escalating situation.
- *Text overlay:* Use captions for all dialogue to ensure accessibility and comprehension.

**3. Payoff** (closing, 5-10 seconds)
End the sketch on a final, definitive punchline that represents an 'absurd but painfully relatable truth' about the object being personified. This final moment should leave the user defeated and the personified object triumphant, encapsulating the core frustration in a hilarious way.
- *Visual:* End on a shot of the user's defeated reaction or the object's final, chillingly funny statement.
- *Audio:* The final line of dialogue should be the strongest punchline, often followed by a moment of silence or a comedic sound effect.
- *Text overlay:* Captions for the final punchline are crucial.


## Pacing
The video should have a clear escalating structure. It starts with a normal premise and steadily builds in absurdity and tension, leading to a final comedic climax. The pacing is conversational, driven by the back-and-forth dialogue.

## Hook Mechanics
The hook is the instant personification of a common, often frustrating, invisible entity. It works by immediately presenting a relatable 'what if' scenario that audiences have likely imagined, making them curious to see how the conversation will play out.

## Visual Style
This is a low-fidelity, character-driven format. It can be shot selfie-style with a simple static camera. The creator can play both roles, using simple props, costumes (like a DIY mask), or different camera angles to distinguish between the characters.

## Audio Strategy
The format is entirely dialogue-driven. The script and performance are key. Background music should be used sparingly to enhance the comedic tone (e.g., overly dramatic or suspenseful music) but should not overpower the conversation.

## Example Topics
- Your Wi-Fi personified as an unreliable roommate who disappears at the worst times.
- Your printer personified as a high-maintenance drama queen who refuses to work.
- Autocorrect personified as an overbearing teacher who thinks they know best.
- Google Maps personified as a passive-aggressive backseat driver.
- Your coffee maker personified as a grumpy person who can't function before its first brew.
