# Format: How [X] Expects Me to [Y]

> A comedic, relatable format that exaggerates the unrealistic expectations a specific person or group (X) has for how you should perform a task (Y). The video shows a sped-up, chaotic, and absurd montage of the creator performing the task at an impossible speed, driven by high-energy music.

**Duration:** 10-25 seconds

## When to Use
Use this format to turn a common professional frustration into a highly shareable, humorous video. It's excellent for building community around shared experiences and creating content that has a high potential to go viral due to its relatability.

## Target Audience
Software engineers, tech leads, product managers, and other tech professionals who want to create humorous, shareable content about the absurdities of developer life, startup culture, and corporate processes.

**Works well for:** Software Development Lifecycle (Debugging, Code Reviews, Deployment), Agile & Project Management Humor (Sprints, Standups, JIRA), Corporate & Office Culture (Slack, Meetings, Performance Reviews), Tech Career & Interviews (Job Hunting, LeetCode, Onboarding), Startup & Hustle Culture

## What Makes It Work
The format's effectiveness stems from extreme relatability. It taps into the universal feeling of having unrealistic expectations placed upon you by a boss, client, or stakeholder. By exaggerating this shared pain point to a comedic extreme, it becomes instantly shareable, as viewers will immediately think of colleagues to send it to.

## Structure

| # | Role | Duration | What to Do |
|---|------|----------|------------|
| 1 | Hook | 1-2 seconds | Immediately present the central premise with a full-screen text overlay. This text hook provides all the context the viewer needs. |
| 2 | Build | 8-20 seconds | Showcase a rapid-fire montage of 3-5 chaotic, exaggerated moments of you performing the task. Each action should be more absurd and frantic than the last, escalating the chaos and demonstrating the impossibility of the expectation. |
| 3 | Payoff | 1-3 seconds | End on the final, most ridiculous action that 'completes' the task. This is the punchline that fulfills the promise of the hook, showing the task being done in a physically impossible way. |


### Section Details

**1. Hook** (opening, 1-2 seconds)
Immediately present the central premise with a full-screen text overlay. This text hook provides all the context the viewer needs.
- *Visual:* The opening shot should establish the setting of the task (e.g., at a desk, in front of a whiteboard). The text should be large and easy to read.
- *Audio:* Start a high-energy, fast-paced, and often trending audio track from the very first frame.
- *Text overlay:* How [X] expects me to [Y] (e.g., 'How my manager expects me to respond to a Slack DM')

**2. Build** (middle, 8-20 seconds)
Showcase a rapid-fire montage of 3-5 chaotic, exaggerated moments of you performing the task. Each action should be more absurd and frantic than the last, escalating the chaos and demonstrating the impossibility of the expectation.
- *Visual:* Use quick cuts, sped-up footage (hyperlapse), and frantic physical actions. The camera can be handheld to add to the sense of chaos. Show yourself multitasking impossibly (e.g., typing with your feet while on a call).
- *Audio:* The music should be the primary audio, driving the frantic pace of the edits and actions. No dialogue or voiceover is necessary.

**3. Payoff** (closing, 1-3 seconds)
End on the final, most ridiculous action that 'completes' the task. This is the punchline that fulfills the promise of the hook, showing the task being done in a physically impossible way.
- *Visual:* This shot should be the peak of the absurdity. Cut the video immediately after the final action is complete to maximize the comedic impact.
- *Audio:* Let the music hit a crescendo or a key beat that aligns with the final action. End the video abruptly.


## Pacing
The pacing is frantic and should continuously accelerate. Start fast and get faster, with the rhythm of the cuts and on-screen actions driven by the tempo of the background music. There are no pauses or moments of rest.

## Hook Mechanics
The hook is entirely based on the on-screen text premise: 'How [X] Expects Me to [Y]'. It works by creating immediate relatability and setting a clear comedic expectation. Viewers instantly recognize the frustrating dynamic and are primed to see it exaggerated.

## Visual Style
The visual style is chaotic, high-energy, and action-oriented. It relies on sped-up footage and a series of quick, jarring cuts. The aesthetic is often low-fi and self-shot, which adds to the authenticity and humor.

## Audio Strategy
Use a trending, high-BPM song or a classic, energetic track that drives the action. The format relies entirely on the music and visuals, so no voiceover or direct-to-camera speaking is needed. The text hook provides all necessary context.

## Common Mistakes
Being too generic; the premise must be specific (e.g., not 'work faster' but 'review a pull request faster'). Dragging out the ending; the video should end immediately after the final payoff action to keep the punchline sharp.

## Example Topics
- How my Product Manager expects me to implement a new feature discovered during standup.
- How my manager expects me to 'quickly' review a 5,000-line pull request before lunch.
- How FAANG interviewers expect me to solve a LeetCode hard on a whiteboard.
- How my brain expects me to fix a bug at 4:59 PM on a Friday.
- How non-tech partners expect me to 'just get off the computer' during an on-call incident.
