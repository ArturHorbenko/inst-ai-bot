# Format: Trying Something I Saw Online

> This format involves finding a viral, impressive, or curious video/concept within your niche and then documenting your own attempt to replicate or adapt it. It positions you as a relatable 'regular person' testing a popular idea, rather than an expert teaching it, creating authentic and curiosity-driven content.

**Duration:** 30-60 seconds

## When to Use
When you want to tap into existing trends without being derivative, show a more vulnerable or experimental side of your personality, or generate content ideas by reacting to the community around you. It's perfect for demonstrating a concept in a practical, entertaining way.

## Target Audience
Software engineers, tech leads, and other tech professionals who are looking for relatable, entertaining, and educational content that reflects their own experiences and curiosities about the industry, tools, and culture.

**Works well for:** Productivity Hacks & Workflows, Coding Challenges & Side Projects, Tool & Framework Tests, Debunking Tech Hype, Career Experiments (e.g., interview styles, resume formats), WFH & Office Culture Commentary

## What Makes It Work
This format works because it's driven by genuine curiosity. If the creator is truly interested in the outcome, the audience will be too. The result (success or failure) is secondary to the entertainment of the attempt, making it a low-pressure, high-reward format that feels authentic and relatable.

### Section Details

**1. Hook** (opening, 3-5 seconds)
State the viral or interesting thing you saw online. Immediately show a clip or screenshot of the original content (e.g., using a green screen or picture-in-picture effect) to provide instant context and credibility.
- *Visual:* Start with a direct-to-camera shot. Quickly transition to a visual of the original content (e.g., screen recording, overlaid video clip).
- *Audio:* Use your natural speaking voice. State the premise clearly: 'I saw someone do [X]...'
- *Text overlay:* Bold title stating the challenge, e.g., 'Trying the Viral 'Code Refactor' Challenge'

**2. Setup** (opening, 3-5 seconds)
Pivot from the original concept to your own, more relatable version of the challenge. State your goal and express genuine curiosity or skepticism about the outcome. This makes the attempt feel authentic.
- *Visual:* Cut back to a direct-to-camera shot. You can be in the environment where you'll perform the attempt (e.g., at your desk).
- *Audio:* Explain your personal twist: '...so I'm going to see if I can do a simplified version using only vanilla JavaScript.'
- *Text overlay:* Text overlay clarifying your specific goal, e.g., 'My Goal: Refactor a 500-line function in under 1 hour.'

**3. Build** (middle, 15-40 seconds)
Show a montage of you performing the attempt. Capture the process, including moments of struggle, small wins, and self-talk. This is where you build tension and tell the story of the journey.
- *Visual:* Use a series of quick cuts, time-lapses, and varied camera angles (e.g., screen recording of code, shot of you thinking, close-up on hands typing).
- *Audio:* Live commentary or a voiceover explaining your thought process. Upbeat, suspenseful, or lo-fi background music can enhance the mood.
- *Text overlay:* Short text callouts to highlight key moments or internal thoughts, e.g., 'Okay, this is harder than it looks,' or 'First bug... classic.'

**4. Payoff** (closing, 3-7 seconds)
Reveal the final outcome of your attempt, whether it was a success or a failure. The video should end almost immediately after the result is clear to provide a satisfying and concise conclusion.
- *Visual:* A clear, final shot showing the result (e.g., the working app, the organized calendar, the finished code). Can end with a reaction shot (e.g., celebration, relief, or humorous defeat).
- *Audio:* A concluding statement about the result. A satisfying sound effect can mark the success or failure.
- *Text overlay:* Text overlay summarizing the result, e.g., 'IT WORKED!' or 'TOTAL FAIL.'

**5. Cta** (closing, 1-2 seconds)
Optionally, add a quick, simple call to action or a final witty remark.
- *Visual:* Can be on the final payoff shot or a quick cut to an end card.
- *Audio:* Voiceover or just music.
- *Text overlay:* 'Follow for more tech experiments' or a question like 'Should I try this again?'


## Pacing
Start with a very fast hook to establish the premise. The middle section should be a dynamic montage of quick cuts to show the process and maintain energy. The ending should be abrupt, revealing the result and concluding quickly without a long explanation.

## Hook Mechanics
The hook leverages 'borrowed authority' and curiosity by referencing an existing viral video or popular concept. By stating 'I saw someone do X,' you immediately create a known premise, and by adding '...so I'm going to try it,' you create a suspenseful question that the viewer wants answered.

## Visual Style
The style is authentic and often feels like a vlog or a personal experiment. Use of screen recordings, point-of-view shots, and direct-to-camera narration makes it feel personal and unscripted. Showing the original clip is crucial for context.

## Audio Strategy
Relies heavily on the creator's personality through direct-to-camera narration and voiceover. Background music should match the tone of the challenge (e.g., epic for a hard challenge, quirky for a funny one). Sound effects can punctuate key moments of success or failure.

## Common Mistakes
Trying to perfectly replicate a feat that is impossible for you. The key is to adapt the challenge to be achievable and relatable, not to prove you're as skilled as the original creator. The value is in the attempt, not necessarily the perfect success.

## Example Topics
- I saw a dev on X claim they built a full-stack app in a weekend with a new AI tool. I'm going to see if I can build a simple to-do list with it in one day.
- My boss shared a productivity article about 'time blocking' every minute of your day. I'm trying it for a day to see if it's genius or a nightmare.
- I saw a video of someone acing a Google system design interview. I'm going to try the same prompt and document my honest, unedited thought process.
- I saw a viral TikTok about 'lazy girl jobs' in tech. As someone who's been feeling burnt out, I'm going to try to structure my day to be as 'lazy' (i.e., efficient) as possible.
- I saw a recipe for a 'programmer's smoothie' that's supposed to boost focus. I'm making it and testing if I can actually solve this LeetCode hard problem after drinking it.
