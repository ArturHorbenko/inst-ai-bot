# Format: The 'What If' Scenario

> A skit-style format that takes a familiar concept, person, or situation from your niche and reimagines it by asking 'What if?'. The video then plays out the absurd, humorous, or surprisingly insightful consequences, making abstract or common experiences highly relatable and entertaining.

**Duration:** 20-45 seconds

## When to Use
Use this format to make a dry or technical topic entertaining, to share a relatable frustration about corporate life, or to build a strong connection with your audience through shared humor. It's excellent for content that aims to be highly shareable.

## Target Audience
Software engineers, tech leads, and corporate professionals who want to create humorous, personality-driven content. Ideal for making niche topics like coding, team dynamics, or career struggles feel relatable and shareable without requiring high production value.

**Works well for:** Developer Humor, Corporate & Office Life, Startup Culture, Programming Language Personification, Tech Career Advice, Mental Health in Tech

## What Makes It Work
The format is effective because it leverages the 'unspoken truth.' By exaggerating a common experience (like a frustrating code review), it validates the audience's feelings, making them feel seen and understood. This high level of relatability makes the content extremely shareable.

### Section Details

**1. Hook** (opening, 1-3 seconds)
Immediately present your 'What If' premise using on-screen text. The premise itself is the hook, creating a strong curiosity loop by promising to show a familiar thing from a completely new and unfamiliar angle.
- *Visual:* A direct-to-camera shot or the first shot of the skit. The focus is on the text overlay being instantly readable.
- *Audio:* Upbeat or intriguing intro music that quickly fades as the first line is spoken.
- *Text overlay:* Use a clear, bold text overlay with a pattern like 'If [X] were [Y]' or 'What if [X] could [Y]?' (e.g., 'If VS Code was my therapist').

**2. Build** (middle, 15-30 seconds)
Deliver on the hook's promise by acting out the scenario. Use a series of short, specific, and recognizable moments that exaggerate an unspoken truth about the topic. Each beat should be a relatable punchline that builds on the central premise.
- *Visual:* Skit format. Use quick cuts between angles or simple costume changes to signify different characters if you're playing multiple roles. The style should be low-fi and authentic, focusing on performance over polish.
- *Audio:* Primarily dialogue-driven. Use sound effects to punctuate jokes and enhance comedic timing. Background music can underscore the mood (e.g., dramatic, tense, silly).

**3. Payoff** (closing, 3-5 seconds)
End with a memorable final beat or punchline that provides a satisfying conclusion to the scenario. This final joke should be one of the strongest and directly tie back to the core truth you're exaggerating.
- *Visual:* A final look to the camera or a conclusive shot that emphasizes the punchline.
- *Audio:* Deliver the final line with strong comedic timing, followed by a punchy sound effect or a moment of silence.
- *Text overlay:* Optional: A simple call-to-action like 'Follow for more honest tech takes' can appear after the final beat.


## Pacing
Fast-paced and energetic. The video should move quickly from the hook into a rapid succession of short scenes or beats. Avoid lingering on any single moment for too long to maintain high retention.

## Hook Mechanics
The hook is the premise itself, delivered via on-screen text. It works by creating a powerful curiosity loop, making the viewer want to see how the absurd or funny scenario plays out. It taps into shared experiences and unspoken truths, making the viewer think, 'I've always wondered about that!'

## Visual Style
Low-production, high-creativity. The format is designed to be filmed easily with a smartphone. It prioritizes a clever premise and good performance over cinematic quality. Simple props, varied camera angles, and quick jump cuts are effective.

## Audio Strategy
Dialogue is the primary driver. Background music should support the comedic or dramatic tone of the skit without overpowering the performance. Use of well-timed sound effects is highly encouraged to enhance punchlines.

## Common Mistakes
Being too vague or only describing the premise instead of acting it out. The humor comes from showing specific, detailed moments. Another mistake is focusing too much on production value; the strength of this format is in the creativity of the premise, not the quality of the camera.

## Example Topics
- If your code compiler could talk back to you during a debugging session.
- What if a Senior Engineer's performance review was written by their pull request comments?
- If Imposter Syndrome was a real person who followed you to stand-up meetings.
- What if your side projects had their own group chat and complained about being abandoned?
- If you had to interview for your own job every six months.
