# Format: What I Learned Doing X

> A direct-to-camera format where the creator shares 3-5 concise, often counter-intuitive lessons learned from a specific, tangible experience (e.g., a project, a time period, a challenge). It establishes credibility upfront and delivers rapid-fire value by distilling a long-term experience into easily digestible insights.

**Duration:** 30-60 seconds

## When to Use
When you want to distill learnings from a significant project, career milestone, or long-term habit into a short, high-value piece of content. Excellent for establishing expertise, sharing actionable advice, and creating highly shareable content that doesn't require a lot of production effort.

## Target Audience
Software engineers, tech leads, engineering managers, and other tech professionals who have accumulated domain-specific experience and want to share practical, hard-won advice with their peers. It's ideal for establishing authority and providing actionable insights without heavy production.

**Works well for:** Career Development, Software Engineering Best Practices, Startup & Side Projects, Tech Leadership & Management, WFH & Productivity, Mental Health in Tech

## What Makes It Work
It provides a cognitive shortcut for the viewer, offering the wisdom of experience without the cost of time and effort. The format's authenticity and directness build trust, while the counter-intuitive nature of the lessons challenges the viewer's assumptions, keeping them engaged.

## Structure

### Section Details

**1. Hook** (opening, 3-5 seconds)
State the specific experience and timeframe to establish credibility and create a curiosity gap. Use the formula: 'I did [Action/Project] for [Time/Quantity], and here's what I learned.'
- *Visual:* Direct-to-camera, medium shot. Feel authentic and conversational, like you're on a video call.
- *Audio:* Clear, confident vocal delivery. No music during the hook.
- *Text overlay:* Headline text summarizing the hook, e.g., 'Crucial things I learned after X.'

**2. Lesson** (middle, 80-90% of total)
Rapidly list 3-5 of your most surprising or counter-intuitive lessons. Keep each point extremely concise (one sentence if possible) and move quickly from one to the next to maintain pacing. Avoid the urge to over-explain.
- *Visual:* Maintain the direct-to-camera shot. Use quick jump cuts between each lesson to eliminate breaths and maintain high energy.
- *Audio:* Voice-led, continuous talking. The pace of your speech drives the video.
- *Text overlay:* Each lesson can appear as a simple text overlay as you say it.

**3. Payoff** (closing, 1-2 seconds)
The video ends immediately after you state the final lesson. Do not add an outro, a CTA, or any filler. The abrupt ending makes the content feel dense with value and encourages re-watches.
- *Visual:* The video simply cuts off after the last word of the final lesson.
- *Audio:* The audio cuts abruptly with the video. No outro music or sound effects.


## Pacing
Fast-paced and information-dense. Use quick jump cuts between each lesson to eliminate all breaths and pauses. The goal is to move from the hook to the first lesson to the last lesson with no lulls or wasted time.

## Hook Mechanics
The hook combines credibility ('I did this specific, challenging thing') with a curiosity gap ('...and here's what I learned'). By quantifying the experience (e.g., 'for a year', '100 times'), it grounds the claim in reality and makes the promised lessons feel more valuable and earned.

## Visual Style
Minimalist and authentic. The style should feel like a FaceTime call with a knowledgeable friend. Avoid complex edits, b-roll, or slick graphics. The focus is entirely on the credibility of the speaker and the value of their insights.

## Audio Strategy
Primarily voice-driven. The creator's natural speech is the main audio. If music is used, it should be a very subtle, non-distracting background track that starts after the hook and doesn't overpower the voice.

## Common Mistakes
Making the lessons too long or over-explaining them, which kills the pacing. Including too many lessons (stick to 3-5). Over-producing the video with fancy edits, which undermines the authentic 'FaceTime' feel. Adding a slow outro or CTA, which dilutes the impact of the abrupt, value-packed ending.

## Example Topics
- What I learned shipping 100+ pull requests at a FAANG company.
- Crucial lessons from launching my SaaS side project in 90 days.
- My biggest takeaways after a year of being a remote-first Engineering Manager.
- What a year of therapy taught me about surviving tech burnout.
- Surprising things I learned after interviewing 50 senior software engineers.
