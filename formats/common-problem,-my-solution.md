# Format: Common Problem, My Solution

> A fast-paced, visually-driven format that presents a widely relatable frustration, shows the process of creating or finding a solution, and culminates in a satisfying reveal. The core mechanic is to build curiosity during the creation phase and deliver a 'wow' moment with a clever, elegant solution.

**Duration:** 10-25 seconds

## When to Use
Excellent for launching a physical product, a SaaS tool, or an open-source project. It's also effective for affiliate marketing by framing a product as the perfect solution to a known problem. Use it to establish yourself as an innovator or builder.

## Target Audience
Software engineers, product makers, and tech creators who want to showcase their problem-solving skills, launch a product or side project, or promote a tool that solves a common industry pain point. It's perfect for builders who want to turn a 'pain point' into compelling content.

**Works well for:** Developer Experience & Tooling, WFH & Desk Setups, Productivity Hacks & Automation, Side Project Showcase, Physical Computing & DIY Tech, Humorous Takes on Dev Life

## What Makes It Work
It provides a complete and satisfying narrative loop in seconds. The viewer's journey from 'I hate that problem' to 'Wow, that solution is genius' creates a strong positive association with the creator and their product. It taps into the simple human desire for problems to be solved elegantly.

### Section Details

**1. Hook (The Problem)** (opening, 2-5 seconds)
Showcase a highly relatable and frustrating problem using quick cuts. Film yourself or use stock/found footage to demonstrate the universality of the annoyance, making the viewer immediately think, 'I have that problem too.'
- *Visual:* Use shaky-cam, user-generated content style, or quick, frustrating cuts to emphasize the annoyance of the problem.
- *Audio:* Match the cuts to the beat of a high-energy, trending song.
- *Text overlay:* When you always see [people experiencing a common problem]...

**2. Build (The Process)** (middle, 4-10 seconds)
Cut to a montage of you actively creating the solution. Show the 'messy middle'—coding on a laptop, tinkering with electronics, sketching designs—without revealing the final product. This builds curiosity and frames you as the problem-solver.
- *Visual:* Use a time-lapse or a series of quick cuts showing different stages of the creation process. Keep the final form obscured or out of focus.
- *Audio:* The music continues, building anticipation towards the reveal.
- *Text overlay:* So you create a solution...

**3. Payoff (The Reveal)** (closing, 4-10 seconds)
Reveal the finished solution in action, perfectly solving the initial problem. This is the satisfying payoff. Show the product being used in its intended context, looking clean, effective, and desirable.
- *Visual:* Use smooth, clean shots. A satisfying close-up of the product, followed by a wider shot of it in use. Slow motion can be used to emphasize the effectiveness.
- *Audio:* The music often hits a crescendo or a satisfying beat drop during the reveal.


## Pacing
Rapid and rhythmic. The video uses quick cuts, often synced to a beat, to move from problem to process to payoff without any downtime. The entire narrative arc is compressed into a very short, high-energy package.

## Hook Mechanics
The hook leverages instant relatability. By presenting a universal frustration, it immediately validates the viewer's own experience, making them feel understood and creating a strong incentive to see the problem solved.

## Visual Style
A 'show, don't tell' approach is critical. The story is conveyed entirely through visuals and minimal on-screen text. The aesthetic shifts from chaotic/frustrating in the beginning, to industrious/mysterious in the middle, to clean/satisfying at the end.

## Audio Strategy
Typically relies on a trending, upbeat audio track to drive the pace and energy. No voiceover is needed as the visual storytelling is self-explanatory. The music choice is key to the video's shareability and overall vibe.

## Common Mistakes
If the problem is abstract (e.g., a software bug) or the solution is non-intuitive, relying solely on visuals can be confusing. In such cases, a simple voiceover or more descriptive on-screen text is necessary to bridge the gap for the viewer.

## Example Topics
- Problem: Cables constantly falling behind your desk. Solution: Designing and 3D printing a custom cable holder.
- Problem: Forgetting complex git commands during a stressful debugging session. Solution: Building a personal CLI tool with simple aliases for common command chains.
- Problem: Your webcam and mic are never right for spontaneous meetings. Solution: Creating a single-device stand that holds your phone as a high-quality webcam with an attached mic.
- Problem: Getting distracted by social media notifications while trying to code. Solution: Building a physical desk light that turns red when a script detects you've opened a distracting website.
- Problem: The tedium of manually updating dependencies in multiple microservices. Solution: Writing a script that automates the entire update and testing process, then showing it run flawlessly.
